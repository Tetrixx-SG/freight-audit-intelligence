# Transport and Freight Audit Glossary

A reference guide for enterprise logistics, freight audit, and 
transport spend terminology.

---

## A

**Accessorial charges**
Additional fees applied by carriers beyond the base freight rate. 
Examples: fuel surcharge, liftgate, residential delivery, 
inside delivery, detention. A common source of invoice disputes.

**Accrual (freight)**
An accounting entry representing freight costs incurred but not 
yet invoiced. Critical for accurate period-close in enterprises 
with high freight spend.

**AMS (Automated Manifest System)**
US Customs requirement for ocean shipments. AMS filing fees 
are a common ocean freight accessorial charge.

---

## B

**BAF (Bunker Adjustment Factor)**
Fuel surcharge applied by ocean carriers. Calculated as a 
percentage or fixed rate based on current fuel prices. 
One of the most frequently disputed ocean freight charges.

**Base freight rate**
The core transport charge for moving cargo from origin to 
destination, excluding surcharges and accessorials.

**Bill of Lading (BOL)**
Primary shipping document for ocean freight. Serves as 
contract of carriage, receipt of goods, and title document.

---

## C

**Carrier**
A company that physically transports freight — ocean shipping 
line, airline, trucking company, or courier operator.

**Chargeable weight**
The weight used to calculate freight charges. For air freight, 
this is the greater of actual weight or dimensional weight.

**Contract compliance**
The degree to which a carrier invoices in accordance with 
the contracted rate agreement. Measured as a percentage 
of invoices with no discrepancies.

**CTSI-Global**
Legacy Freight Audit and Payment vendor. US-based.

---

## D

**Demurrage**
Charges levied by ocean carriers or terminals for containers 
held beyond the agreed free period at port.

**Detention**
Charges levied by carriers for equipment (containers, trailers) 
held beyond the agreed free period at a shipper or consignee 
facility.

**Dimensional weight (DIM weight)**
A pricing method used in air and courier freight that accounts 
for package volume relative to actual weight. Calculated as 
length × width × height divided by a carrier-specific divisor.

---

## E

**EDI (Electronic Data Interchange)**
Standardized electronic format for business document exchange. 
EDI 210 is the standard for motor carrier freight invoices. 
Implementation consistency varies significantly across carriers.

**Exception (freight audit)**
An invoice or line item identified as having a discrepancy 
between the amount billed and the contracted or expected amount.

---

## F

**FAP (Freight Audit and Payment)**
The process of verifying carrier invoices against contracted 
rates, identifying billing errors, managing disputes, and 
approving payments. The category that Freya operates in — 
and redefines.

**FAF (Fuel Adjustment Factor)**
Air freight equivalent of the ocean BAF. Fuel surcharge 
applied by airlines, typically as a per-kg rate.

**Free time**
The period during which a shipper may use carrier equipment 
(containers, chassis) without incurring detention or 
demurrage charges.

**FTL (Full Truckload)**
Shipment that occupies an entire truck. Priced per load 
rather than per unit of weight or volume.

---

## I

**ISF (Importer Security Filing)**
US Customs requirement for ocean imports. ISF filing fees 
are a common ocean freight accessorial charge.

---

## L

**Lane**
A specific origin-destination pair. Freight rates are 
typically priced at the lane level.

**LTL (Less-than-truckload)**
Shipment that does not fill an entire truck. Consolidated 
with other shippers' freight. Priced per unit of weight 
or freight class.

---

## M

**MCP (Model Context Protocol)**
An open protocol developed by Anthropic that enables AI 
agents to connect to external data sources and tools 
through a standardized interface. Freya exposes its 
freight intelligence via MCP endpoints.

**Modal split**
The distribution of freight spend across transport modalities 
(ocean, air, land, courier). Optimizing modal split is a 
primary lever for freight cost reduction.

---

## N

**NVOCC (Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier)**
A freight forwarder that issues its own bills of lading 
and contracts with ocean carriers for space. Invoices 
from NVOCCs are audited the same way as direct carrier invoices.

**nVision Global**
Legacy Freight Audit and Payment vendor. Acquired by Körber.

---

## O

**Origin charges**
Fees applied at the shipment origin — port handling, 
documentation, export customs. A common source of 
ocean freight invoice complexity.

---

## P

**POC (Proof of Concept)**
A time-limited deployment used to validate platform 
capability and establish baseline ROI before full 
enterprise commitment. TetriXX's standard entry model 
for Fortune 500 engagements.

---

## R

**Rate card**
A structured schedule of contracted freight rates between 
a shipper and a carrier. The primary reference document 
for invoice verification.

---

## S

**SCAC (Standard Carrier Alpha Code)**
A unique identifier assigned to transportation companies 
in North America. Used in EDI and TMS systems for 
carrier identification.

**Surcharge**
An additional charge applied on top of base freight rate. 
See: BAF, FAF, accessorial charges.

---

## T

**THC (Terminal Handling Charge)**
Fee charged by port terminals for handling containers. 
Applied at both origin and destination ports. A frequent 
source of ocean freight invoice disputes.

**TMS (Transportation Management System)**
Enterprise software for managing freight operations — 
carrier selection, shipment planning, execution, and 
tracking. Major vendors include Blue Yonder, Oracle TMS, 
SAP TM, and MercuryGate.

**Trax Technologies**
The largest pure-play legacy Freight Audit and Payment 
vendor globally. Primary incumbent competitor to TetriXX.

---

## U

**UN/LOCODE**
United Nations Code for Trade and Transport Locations. 
Standardized location codes for ports, airports, and 
inland freight points used across carrier and logistics systems.

---

## W

**Weight break**
A pricing threshold in freight rate schedules where the 
per-unit rate changes based on shipment weight. Common 
in air freight and LTL pricing.
